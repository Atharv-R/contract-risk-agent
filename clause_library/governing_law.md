# Governing Law and Dispute Resolution Clauses

## What It Is
Governing law clauses determine which jurisdiction's laws apply to interpret the contract. Dispute resolution clauses specify how conflicts will be resolved — through courts, arbitration, or mediation.

## Standard and Acceptable
- Governing law of a neutral or mutually convenient jurisdiction
- Governing law of the jurisdiction where the primary work is performed
- Choice of jurisdiction that has a reasonable connection to the parties or transaction
- Option for mediation before litigation or arbitration
- Mutual agreement on arbitration rules and arbitrator selection
- Each party bears its own legal costs unless the case is frivolous
- Reasonable venue that does not create undue burden for either party

## Red Flags
- Governing law of a foreign country with no connection to either party
- Mandatory binding arbitration with no option for court proceedings
- Arbitrator selection controlled by only one party
- Dispute resolution in a location far from one party creating significant disadvantage
- Waiver of jury trial rights
- Loser-pays provisions that discourage legitimate claims
- Very short statute of limitations for bringing claims
- Prohibition on class action or collective claims
- Exclusive jurisdiction in a specific court that heavily favors one party
- No choice of law clause at all (creates uncertainty)

## Example: Acceptable Language
"This Agreement shall be governed by the laws of the State of California. The parties agree to first attempt mediation of any dispute. If mediation fails, disputes shall be resolved by binding arbitration under AAA Commercial Rules, with the arbitrator mutually selected by the parties."

## Example: Problematic Language
"This Agreement shall be governed by the laws of the State of Delaware. Any disputes shall be resolved exclusively through binding arbitration in Wilmington, Delaware, with the arbitrator selected solely by Company. Each party waives all right to a jury trial."

## Jurisdiction Notes
- Delaware and New York are commonly chosen for US commercial contracts due to well-developed business law
- Choice of law provisions are generally enforceable but courts may refuse to apply foreign law contrary to local public policy
- Federal Arbitration Act strongly favors enforcement of arbitration agreements
- Some consumer protection statutes cannot be waived by choice of law provisions
- International contracts should specify UNCITRAL or ICC arbitration rules
