# Liability and Limitation of Liability Clauses

## What It Is
Liability clauses define the maximum financial exposure each party faces under the contract. A liability cap sets a ceiling on damages one party can claim from the other. These clauses also address what types of damages are recoverable (direct, indirect, consequential, etc.).

## Standard and Acceptable
- Mutual liability cap applying equally to both parties
- Cap set at a reasonable amount, typically 12 months of fees paid or total contract value
- Exclusion of indirect and consequential damages is standard for both parties
- Carve-outs from the cap for specific high-risk items (IP infringement, confidentiality breach, willful misconduct) are normal
- Each party's cap is proportional to its role and risk

## Red Flags
- One party has uncapped or unlimited liability while the other has a low cap
- Liability cap set at an unreasonably low amount (e.g., 3 months fees) for one side
- No cap at all on liability for one or both parties
- Consequential damages excluded for one party but not the other
- Inclusion of punitive or exemplary damages
- Liability cap does not reset per incident or per year (lifetime cap can be exhausted quickly)
- Cap set at fees paid in very short lookback period (e.g., last 1-3 months)

## Example: Acceptable Language
"NEITHER PARTY'S TOTAL AGGREGATE LIABILITY UNDER THIS AGREEMENT SHALL EXCEED THE TOTAL FEES PAID OR PAYABLE IN THE TWELVE (12) MONTHS PRECEDING THE CLAIM. NEITHER PARTY SHALL BE LIABLE FOR ANY INDIRECT, INCIDENTAL, CONSEQUENTIAL, OR PUNITIVE DAMAGES."

## Example: Problematic Language
"SERVICE PROVIDER SHALL BE LIABLE FOR ALL DAMAGES OF ANY KIND, INCLUDING CONSEQUENTIAL AND PUNITIVE DAMAGES, WITHOUT ANY CAP OR LIMITATION. COMPANY'S LIABILITY SHALL NOT EXCEED FEES PAID IN THE PRIOR THREE (3) MONTHS."

## Jurisdiction Notes
- Some jurisdictions do not allow limitation of liability for gross negligence or willful misconduct
- Consumer contracts may have statutory minimum liability requirements
- UCC Article 2 governs limitations of liability for sale of goods
- Courts may strike unconscionable liability provisions even in B2B contexts
