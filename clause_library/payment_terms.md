# Payment Terms Clauses

## What It Is
Payment terms clauses specify when and how payments are made, including invoicing procedures, payment windows, late fees, disputed invoice processes, and currency.

## Standard and Acceptable
- Net 30 payment terms (invoice due within 30 days of receipt)
- Clear invoicing schedule (monthly, milestone-based, or upon delivery)
- Late fee of 1-1.5% per month or the lesser of the stated rate and the legal maximum
- Right to dispute invoices in good faith without triggering late fees
- Clear currency specification
- Expense reimbursement with reasonable documentation requirements
- Right to suspend services for non-payment after written notice and cure period
- Payment upon completion of milestones for project-based work

## Red Flags
- Net 60 or Net 90 payment terms (cash flow burden on service provider)
- Excessively high late fees (above 2% per month)
- No right to dispute invoices without triggering default
- Payment contingent on undefined client satisfaction or acceptance
- No interest on late payments
- Right to withhold payment indefinitely for vague reasons
- Automatic deduction of penalties from invoices without prior notice
- Payment only upon project completion with no milestone payments for long projects
- Set-off rights allowing deduction of unrelated claims from payments

## Example: Acceptable Language
"Company shall pay undisputed invoices within thirty (30) days of receipt. Late payments shall accrue interest at 1% per month. Either party may dispute an invoice in good faith by providing written notice within fifteen (15) days of receipt."

## Example: Problematic Language
"All invoices are payable Net 90. Late payments accrue interest at 3% per month. Company may withhold payment for any reason pending final acceptance of all Deliverables. Company may offset any amounts owed to Service Provider against any claims Company has against Service Provider."

## Jurisdiction Notes
- Prompt Payment Acts in many states set maximum payment terms for government contracts
- Some jurisdictions cap late payment interest rates
- EU Late Payment Directive sets minimum standards for commercial transactions in Europe
- UCC governs payment terms for sale of goods
