# Non-Compete and Non-Solicitation Clauses

## What It Is
Non-compete clauses restrict a party from engaging in competitive business activities during and after the contract term. Non-solicitation clauses prevent hiring or soliciting the other party's employees or customers.

## Standard and Acceptable
- Duration of 6-12 months post-termination for non-competes
- Geographic scope limited to specific markets where the company actually operates
- Narrowly defined competitive activities directly related to the contract scope
- Non-solicitation of 12 months for employees is generally reasonable
- Restrictions proportional to the party's access to confidential information
- Some form of compensation or consideration during the restricted period

## Red Flags
- Duration exceeding 12-24 months post-termination
- Nationwide or international geographic scope with no logical connection to actual business
- Overly broad definition of competitive activities (any business "similar to" the company)
- Applied to independent contractors or service providers rather than employees
- No geographic limitation at all
- Restriction applies regardless of who terminates or why
- No consideration or compensation during the restricted period
- Covers activities unrelated to the services performed under the contract
- Includes non-solicitation of customers the party had relationships with before the contract

## Example: Acceptable Language
"During the term and for twelve (12) months after termination, Service Provider shall not provide substantially similar services to the specific competitors listed in Exhibit A within the metropolitan areas where Company operates."

## Example: Problematic Language
"Service Provider shall not engage in any competitive activities anywhere within North America for a period of thirty-six (36) months following termination, including any business that is similar to or competes with any aspect of Company's business."

## Jurisdiction Notes
- California broadly prohibits non-compete agreements (CA Business & Professions Code 16600) with very limited exceptions
- Colorado, Illinois, Oregon, and Washington have significant restrictions on non-competes
- FTC has proposed rules to ban non-competes nationally (pending litigation as of 2024)
- Many states require additional consideration beyond continued employment
- Non-competes for independent contractors are harder to enforce than for employees
- Courts generally apply reasonableness tests considering duration, geography, and scope
