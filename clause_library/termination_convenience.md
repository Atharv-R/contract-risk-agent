# Termination Clauses

## What It Is
Termination clauses define how and when each party can end the contract. They include termination for cause (breach), termination for convenience (no reason needed), notice requirements, and what happens after termination.

## Standard and Acceptable
- Mutual termination for cause with a reasonable cure period (30 days is standard)
- Mutual termination for convenience with reasonable notice (30-90 days)
- Clear definition of what constitutes material breach
- Payment for work completed up to termination date
- Reasonable transition period with cooperation obligations
- Data return and destruction obligations are clearly defined
- Survival clause specifying which sections outlast termination

## Red Flags
- Only one party can terminate for convenience while the other cannot
- Very short or no cure period for breach (less than 15 days)
- Termination for convenience with very short notice (less than 15 days)
- Termination penalties or fees that are disproportionate
- No payment for work completed but not yet delivered
- Requirement to provide transition assistance at own expense
- One party can terminate immediately while the other needs 90 days notice
- Post-termination obligations that are unreasonably burdensome
- No termination right at all for one party
- Automatic forfeiture of unpaid fees upon termination

## Example: Acceptable Language
"Either party may terminate this Agreement for convenience upon sixty (60) days written notice. Either party may terminate for cause if the other party materially breaches and fails to cure within thirty (30) days of written notice. Upon termination, Company shall pay for all Services performed through the termination date."

## Example: Problematic Language
"Company may terminate at any time upon 15 days notice. Service Provider may not terminate for convenience and must complete all work regardless of circumstances. Upon termination, Service Provider shall provide 60 days of transition assistance at its own expense."

## Jurisdiction Notes
- Government contracts often have specific termination for convenience rights under FAR
- Some jurisdictions imply a reasonable notice period even when the contract is silent
- Employment-related contracts may be subject to at-will termination laws
- Termination provisions interact with bankruptcy law (Section 365 of Bankruptcy Code)
