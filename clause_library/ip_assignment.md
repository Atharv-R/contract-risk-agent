# Intellectual Property Assignment Clauses

## What It Is
IP assignment clauses determine who owns the intellectual property, inventions, and work product created during the contract. They can transfer ownership from the creator to the hiring party, often using "work for hire" doctrine.

## Standard and Acceptable
- Clear definition of what constitutes project-specific deliverables vs. general knowledge
- Pre-existing IP is explicitly carved out and retained by the creator
- Assignment is limited to work product specifically created under the contract
- Creator retains rights to general tools, methodologies, and frameworks developed independently
- License-back provision allowing creator to use general concepts in future work
- Assignment limited to the specific project scope defined in SOW

## Red Flags
- Blanket assignment of ALL intellectual property, inventions, and ideas (not just project deliverables)
- No carve-out for pre-existing IP
- Assignment covers work conceived "in connection with" rather than "in performance of" the agreement (overly broad)
- Moral rights waiver (common but aggressive)
- Assignment of future IP not yet created
- No license-back for pre-existing IP incorporated into deliverables
- "Further assurances" clause requiring unlimited cooperation to perfect IP rights
- Assignment survives termination with no time limit
- Covers IP created outside of working hours or outside the project scope

## Example: Acceptable Language
"Deliverables specifically created under a Statement of Work shall be assigned to Company upon full payment. Service Provider retains all right, title, and interest in pre-existing IP and general tools, methodologies, and frameworks. Company receives a perpetual license to pre-existing IP embedded in Deliverables."

## Example: Problematic Language
"All work product, inventions, discoveries, and improvements, whether or not patentable, conceived or developed by Service Provider during the term of this Agreement, whether or not related to the Services, shall be the sole property of Company."

## Jurisdiction Notes
- US Copyright Act "work for hire" doctrine only applies to specific categories; assignment language is needed as backup
- Some jurisdictions require additional consideration for IP assignment beyond the contract fees
- California Labor Code 2870 limits employer IP assignment for inventions developed on own time
- Assignment of moral rights is not possible in some civil law jurisdictions (France, Germany)
