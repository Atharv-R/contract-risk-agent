# Confidentiality and Non-Disclosure Clauses

## What It Is
Confidentiality clauses protect sensitive information shared between parties during the contract. They define what constitutes confidential information, how it must be handled, and how long the obligations last.

## Standard and Acceptable
- Mutual confidentiality obligations applying equally to both parties
- Clear, reasonable definition of what constitutes confidential information
- Standard exceptions: publicly available information, independently developed, prior knowledge, legally required disclosure
- Duration of 2-5 years after termination for general confidential information
- Trade secrets protected indefinitely (or as long as they remain trade secrets)
- Obligation to return or destroy confidential information upon termination
- Permitted disclosure to employees and advisors on a need-to-know basis with similar obligations

## Red Flags
- Perpetual confidentiality obligations for ALL information (not just trade secrets)
- Overly broad definition that could include general industry knowledge
- One-sided obligations where only one party must maintain confidentiality
- No standard exceptions carved out
- No ability to disclose to legal or financial advisors
- Requirement to notify disclosing party before complying with legal compulsion (may conflict with law)
- Liquidated damages for any breach regardless of actual harm
- No obligation to return confidential information after termination
- Residuals clause absent (no right to use general knowledge retained in memory)

## Example: Acceptable Language
"Each party shall hold the other's Confidential Information in confidence for a period of three (3) years following disclosure, except for trade secrets which shall be protected for as long as they remain trade secrets."

## Example: Problematic Language
"Service Provider shall maintain the confidentiality of all Company information in perpetuity. Any breach shall result in liquidated damages of $500,000 per occurrence."

## Jurisdiction Notes
- Defend Trade Secrets Act (DTSA) provides federal trade secret protection in the US
- GDPR in Europe imposes specific confidentiality requirements for personal data
- Some jurisdictions require that confidentiality obligations be reasonable in scope and duration
- Whistleblower protections may override confidentiality obligations
