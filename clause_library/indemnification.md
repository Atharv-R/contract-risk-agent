# Indemnification Clauses

## What It Is
Indemnification clauses require one party to compensate the other for losses, damages, or legal costs arising from specific events. They shift risk by making one party financially responsible for certain claims.

## Standard and Acceptable
- Mutual indemnification where both parties indemnify each other for their own breaches
- Each party indemnifies for their own negligence or willful misconduct
- IP infringement indemnification from the delivering party is standard
- Clear definition of what triggers indemnification obligations
- Reasonable process: notice requirements, right to control defense, cooperation obligations
- Cap on indemnification aligned with the overall liability cap
- Duty to mitigate damages

## Red Flags
- One-sided indemnification where only one party bears all risk
- Obligation to indemnify for the OTHER party's negligence or actions
- No cap on indemnification obligations even when overall liability is capped
- Indemnification triggered by broad events rather than specific breaches
- No right to control or participate in the defense of claims
- Indemnification for "any and all claims arising from or related to" the agreement (too broad)
- No notice requirements before indemnification kicks in
- Explicit statement that the other party has NO indemnification obligations

## Example: Acceptable Language
"Each party shall indemnify, defend, and hold harmless the other party from claims arising from the indemnifying party's (a) material breach of this Agreement, (b) negligence or willful misconduct, or (c) violation of applicable law."

## Example: Problematic Language
"Service Provider shall defend, indemnify, and hold harmless Company from any and all claims, damages, and expenses of any kind arising from this Agreement. Company shall have no obligation to indemnify Service Provider under any circumstances."

## Jurisdiction Notes
- Anti-indemnity statutes in many states restrict indemnification for one's own negligence (construction industry especially)
- Some jurisdictions require clear and unequivocal language for broad indemnification
- Insurance coverage may be required to back indemnification obligations
- Indemnification obligations may survive contract termination indefinitely unless limited
