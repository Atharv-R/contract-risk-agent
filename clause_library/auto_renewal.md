# Auto-Renewal Clauses

## What It Is
Auto-renewal clauses automatically extend a contract for additional terms unless one party actively opts out by providing written notice within a specified window. They are sometimes called "evergreen" clauses.

## Standard and Acceptable
- Renewal periods match the initial term or are shorter (e.g., 12-month initial, 12-month renewals)
- Notice window of 30-60 days before renewal date is reasonable
- Both parties have equal right to opt out
- Terms remain the same upon renewal, or changes require mutual agreement
- Clear, conspicuous language about the auto-renewal mechanism

## Red Flags
- Very short notice window (less than 30 days) making it easy to miss the cancellation deadline
- Long renewal periods (multi-year auto-renewals)
- Only one party has the right to prevent renewal
- Pricing or terms can change unilaterally upon renewal
- Auto-renewal buried in dense text without clear disclosure
- No cap on the number of renewal cycles
- Renewal notice must be sent to an obscure address or specific individual

## Example: Acceptable Language
"This Agreement shall renew for successive one (1) year periods unless either party provides written notice of non-renewal at least sixty (60) days prior to the end of the then-current term."

## Example: Problematic Language
"This Agreement shall automatically renew for successive three (3) year periods unless the Service Provider provides written notice via certified mail to Company's registered agent at least ninety (90) days prior to expiration. Company reserves the right to adjust fees and terms upon renewal."

## Jurisdiction Notes
- California requires clear and conspicuous disclosure of auto-renewal terms in consumer contracts (CA Business & Professions Code 17600-17606)
- Many US states have enacted auto-renewal disclosure laws for consumer agreements
- B2B contracts have fewer statutory protections but courts may scrutinize unconscionable terms
- Illinois, New York, and several other states have specific auto-renewal notification requirements
